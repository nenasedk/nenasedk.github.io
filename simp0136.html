Observing SIMP-0136 using the James Webb Space telescope

We observed SIMP-0136 using NIRSpec/PRISM and MIRI/LRS. Using time-series observations, we observed one full 2.4 hour period using each instrument. This provided over 6000 individual spectra, and provided wavelength coverage from 0.9-11 micron at high signal-to-noise.

Wavelength dependent variability

The atmosphere of SIMP-0136 exhibits complex, wavelength dependent variability. Different physical processes cause the atmospheric state to change in uniquely identifiable ways. These changes are thought to be due to small variations in the temperature and cloud coverage. This changes the amount and colour of light that is emitted from the object.

Variability maps

Similar to McCarthy et al. (2025), we map the changing spectrum as a function of both wavelength and time.

Time resolved retrievals with petitRADTRANS

Atmospheric retrievals are a key tool for inferring the properties of exoplanet and brown dwarf atmospheres. Using petitRADTRANS, we performed atmospheric retrievals on spectra sampled every 15 degrees of rotation. We fit for the pressure-temperature profile, chemical abundances, and cloud properties. With the retrievals complete, we could examine how each parameter varied with time.

Aurora on another world

SIMP-0136 is a radio-pulsing object, which is generally attributed to auroral activity on brown dwarfs. We observe an inversion in the upper atmosphere, similar to that on Jupiter, which we attribute to heating from electron precipitation and joule heating from the aurora.

Taking SIMP's temperature

We find that most of the observed variability is explained by changes in the temperature of the troposphere. These small changes in temperature lead to a variation of about 5 K from the coldest longitudes to the warmest.

Methane as a thermometer

The upper atmosphere is probed by the 3.3 micron methane absorption feature, as well as further methane and water absorption in the mid infrared. Similar to the methane emission seen in WISE-, this allows us to infer the presence of an inversion at 1 mbar.

Constant clouds

While clouds are often pointed to as a driver of variability. However, on SIMP-0136 we find that the data is best-fit with a model that keeps the cloud parameters constant, and the changes in the temperature structure and chemistry fully explain the observed variability. The clouds are found to be patchy, but this patchiness is homogeneous at all longitudes.
